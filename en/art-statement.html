Artist Statement

I live and work in Saint Petersburg. I have been working with generative art since 2021.

I work with video, animation and installation. I am interested in how the continuous flow of images changes our perception: what we notice, what we stop feeling, and how we distinguish our own experiences from those imposed on us. My work explores information overload, memory and everyday life: how familiar images shape our sense of the world, or prevent us from seeing it.

My materials include pop culture, memes, everyday scenes, gestures and phrases. Through transformations, repetition and unexpected combinations, I look for other ways of reading them. An ordinary polite phrase can unfold into a natural landscape, a funny character can become a way of speaking about hope, and an endless succession of images can reveal the repetition of a single state.

I value sincerity that leaves room for irony. I do not want to ignore anxiety and contradictions, but neither do I want hopelessness to become the only way of speaking about the present. For me, humour is a way of staying connected to the world without dismissing its complexity. I want to notice the warmth, curiosity and simple joy of living that remain beneath the noise.

I combine code, 3D, neural-network tools, compositing and editing. Technical complexity is not an end in itself: I choose my methods to suit the idea, movement and rhythm of a work. I am also interested in a contradiction within my practice: I use image-making technologies to speak, among other things, about exhaustion caused by their excess.

I aim for a clear visual language that leaves room for the viewer. I do not want to explain everything or insist on a single correct interpretation. I want to leave enough space for someone to recognise their own experience, imagine the rest of a story, or briefly step outside the familiar flow and notice what they feel.

How I Came to Work with Neural Networks

In 2021, I began making generative art and visualisations with JavaScript. I published works on NFT platforms, including fxhash. In 2022–2023, I became part of the NFT movement and started exploring emerging neural-network tools.

I started with Disco Diffusion, then worked with early versions of Midjourney. In Midjourney, I looked for prompt glitches: combinations of words that produced unexpected images and revealed the tool’s own visual language.

With the arrival of Stable Diffusion, I became increasingly interested in controlling generation. I used ControlNet, trained my own LoRAs, and explored animation with AnimateDiff. I studied early versions of ComfyUI and gradually made it the main tool in my practice.

This path brought together programming, image experimentation and movement. I now combine them in video art, animation and installations, choosing tools to suit each idea.
